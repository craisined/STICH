A machine-learning project

Turn humming into classical music—and back.

STICH is an AI that translates between two musical worlds: the tune you hum and a richer classical rendition of it. Hum a melody, hear it reimagined. Play a classical phrase, hear how someone might hum it.

What is it?

Think of a translator — but for music.

When you translate a sentence from English to Spanish, the meaning stays the same but the words change. STICH does the same thing with music. It takes a simple hummed melody and re-voices it in a classical style — and it can go the other direction too.

The clever part: nobody handed it matched examples. We never showed it "this exact hum equals this exact classical piece." It learned the style of each world on its own and figured out how to travel between them.

How it works

One map of sound, and a guide across it.

🗺

The map

An autoencoder learns to boil any clip down to a short list of numbers — a point on a map of sound — and to rebuild the audio from that point again.

🎭

The forger

A second network learns to move a point from the humming region of that map to the classical one — trained against a critic whose whole job is to spot points that only pretend to be classical.

🔁

The round trip

A matching network moves points back the other way, and the pair is scored on whether a hum survives the trip out and home again. That is what keeps your melody intact while the style changes.

In machine-learning terms: an autoencoder over raw audio with a CycleGAN operating on its latent embeddings — two generators, one per direction, trained adversarially under a cycle-consistency loss. Still no paired examples.

Model
Autoencoder over raw audio; style transfer is a CycleGAN over its latent embeddings.
The latent space
A 10-second clip — 480,000 samples — becomes 16 channels × 234 frames. The generators only ever work in here, never on the waveform.
Generators
One per direction, ≈1.1 M parameters each: five residual blocks of wide 1-D convolutions, kept at full length end to end.
Discriminators
A 38 K-parameter 1-D patch critic per domain, judging stretches of the embedding rather than the clip as a whole.
Training signal
Adversarial loss, plus cycle-consistency and identity terms — what keeps the melody through the translation.
Humming data
HumTrans — 14,614 processed clips of recorded hummed melodies.
Classical data
MusicNet — 12,126 processed clips from classical recordings.
Audio format
Mono 10-second clips — 16 kHz on disk, resampled to 48 kHz for the model, which returns 48 kHz.
This site
Plain HTML, CSS & JavaScript, with a Flask server running PyTorch for the model.
